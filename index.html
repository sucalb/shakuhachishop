一音成仏

The way of the bamboo

Enlightenment in a single sound

For the komusō monks of Edo Japan the shakuhachi was not an instrument for performance. It was a practice. One breath, one note, fully given; the saying ichion jōbutsu holds that a single true sound is enough.

虚無僧

Komusō

The “monks of emptiness” of the Fuke sect, who walked through Edo Japan under woven basket hats, playing for alms.

本曲

Honkyoku

The “original pieces”: solo music handed from teacher to student, made for meditation rather than for an audience.

吹禅

Suizen

“Blowing Zen”. The breath becomes the practice, and a single long tone is a meditation of its own.

間

Ma

The space between notes. Silence belongs to the music, as the empty paper belongs to an ink painting.

A quick guide

Choosing a length

Shakuhachi are named by length in shaku, about 30.3 cm. A longer flute plays lower: each extra tenth of a shaku is roughly a half step down. The 1.8 in D is the standard flute; long flutes are deep, slow and meditative.
